METHOD (handoff Task 6). Inject a systematic bias of X ms into one
device's breath durations, re-run the direction-correct classification,
and take the X at which between-device classification agreement falls
below an acceptable level. That X is the CEILING on the margin.

NOTE ON WHAT THE DERIVATION IS FOR. It answers 'at what bias do downstream
conclusions start to change', which bounds what may be TOLERATED. It does
not say what should be CLAIMED. The preregistered margin is set tighter,
since the design supports far more precision than the ceiling requires.

DECISION STATISTIC: Gwet's AC1, lower 95% bound, floor 0.8 

Cohen's kappa was the original rule (docs/discrepancies.md C3) and is
DEGENERATE for this design. With a direction-correct base rate near 0.95,
chance agreement is nearly as high as observed agreement, so kappa reads
about +0.19 at ZERO injected bias, already far below any usable floor,
and it is NON-MONOTONIC in the bias it is meant to track. C3 preregistered
AC1 and percent agreement as companions precisely so a prevalence artefact
would be diagnosable rather than fatal. It was diagnosed; AC1 is promoted
to the decision rule and kappa is retained as a descriptive.
Approved by Norm 2026-08-07.

BASE RATE is assumed, not taken from the pilot (Section 5.12 note 5).
Study 5's data is not vendored, so no empirical value exists; the rate is
derived from the design and from the breath-duration noise the pilot did
license, with adherence assumed at 0.8
